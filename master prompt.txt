# **Comprehensive Summary: Smart Healthcare Robot**
**Project Title:** Smart Healthcare Robot: Bringing Care Closer to Patients in Oman  
**Institution:** Sultan Qaboos University, Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering  
**Authors:** Almoulla Talal Almaawali & Abdulmunim Khamis Albusaidi  
**Supervisor:** Dr. Ahmed Chehib Ammari  
**Date:** December 2025 (Academic Year 2025–2026)

---

## **1. Project Overview and Introduction**
Modern healthcare systems face critical challenges, including staff shortages, the logistical burden of non-clinical tasks, and the risk of infection during routine contact. In Oman, geographic distances create additional barriers to providing timely care to rural populations. 

This project details the conceptual design and system architecture of a **Smart Healthcare Robot** tailored for Omani hospitals. The core objective is to design a holistic system that merges three distinct domains into a single platform:
1. **Autonomous indoor navigation** to independently handle hospital logistics.
2. **High-fidelity telepresence** to allow remote clinicians to consult with patients.
3. **Conversational AI** (utilizing Large Language Models) to provide patient engagement, triage, and guidance.

**Key Project Objectives:**
* Develop a robust autonomous navigation system using LiDAR-based Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM).
* Engineer a secure, low-latency audio/visual telepresence interface for remote doctors.
* Integrate an AI interaction layer for natural dialogue in both English and Arabic.
* Validate the system in a simulated pilot scenario (e.g., nurse station to patient bed).

The project is structured to span two semesters (Sept 2025 – May 2026), moving from planning and design to implementation, testing, and finalization.

---

## **2. Literature Review and Technical Building Blocks**
The authors analyzed current robotic successes in consumer (vacuum cleaners), industrial (Amazon fulfillment), and healthcare markets to establish baseline capabilities. 

### **The Healthcare Gap**
Current medical robotics are heavily fragmented:
* **Telepresence robots** (e.g., InTouch RP-VITA) enable remote consultations but often require manual driving and feel impersonal.
* **Socially Assistive Robots (SARs)** (e.g., PARO, NAO) provide excellent patient engagement but lack clinical mobility and telepresence.
* **Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs)** (e.g., PuduBot 2, Keenon M2) excel at logistics and disinfection but offer limited human interaction.

This project addresses the research gap by combining **clinical access**, **patient-centric engagement**, and **operational independence** into one cost-effective system.

### **Technical Technologies Explored:**
* **Navigation:** The authors evaluated RF localization methods like Ultra-Wideband (UWB) and Bluetooth Angle of Arrival (AoA), as well as Visual/Laser SLAM. They determined that fusing 2D LiDAR with an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) and wheel odometry yields the most robust navigation in dynamic hospital corridors.
* **Conversational AI:** Leading LLMs were compared based on December 2025 pricing. Specialized models like Google's Med-Gemma are too heavy for simple patient interactions. Ultimately, models with vast context windows and cost-efficiencies (like Gemini and DeepSeek) were prioritized.

---

## **3. Requirements and Engineering Constraints**
To guide design and validation, the project formalizes strict customer and engineering specifications.

### **Customer Requirements (CRs):**
* **CR1:** Autonomous navigation around static/dynamic obstacles.
* **CR2:** Remote high-quality video and audio telepresence.
* **CR3:** Natural language interaction for patient engagement.
* **CR4:** Infection control (easy-to-clean surfaces).
* **CR5:** User-friendly interfaces for patients and staff.
* **CR6:** Continuous operation and autonomous battery charging.

### **Engineering Targets (ERs):**
* **Position Accuracy:** $\pm 20\text{ cm}$ in mapped environments.
* **Velocity:** $0.5\text{ to }1.0\text{ m/s}$ in corridors (compliant with ISO 13482 safety standards).
* **Telepresence:** Minimum 720p resolution at 30 fps; latency $<200\text{ ms}$.
* **AI Latency:** Conversational responses within 3 seconds.
* **Uptime:** $4\text{ to }6\text{ hours}$ of continuous operation; minimum $95\%$ docking success rate.

### **Engineering Constraints (ECs):**
* **Budget:** A strict cap of **\$2,500 USD** (traditional commercial hospital robots cost \$12,000–\$47,500).
* **Timeline:** Completion within one academic year (9–10 months).
* **Infrastructure:** Must utilize standard hospital Wi-Fi and accommodate standard Omani corridor widths ($\ge 1.5\text{ m}$).

---

## **4. System Conceptual Design and Selection**
The authors systematically evaluated three structural alternatives using the **Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)** decision-making matrix.

### **The Three Alternatives:**
* **Alternative A (Commercial Pre-Built Platform):** High cost, low customization, but fast development and certified safety.
* **Alternative B (Custom-Built Modular Platform):** Very low cost, maximum flexibility, but high technical risk and longest development time.
* **Alternative C (Hybrid Pre-Built Base with Custom Intelligence):** Merges a commercially proven base with student-developed intelligence layers. 

**Decision:** While Alternative A scored highest mathematically, **Alternative C (Hybrid)** was selected because it perfectly balanced reliability and cost, while allowing the students to innovate on the human-robot interaction (HRI) layer.

### **Component Selection via AHP:**
* **Robot Platform:** TurtleBot 4 or Yahboom ROS-MASTER X3.
* **Computing Platform:** **Raspberry Pi 5** (chosen over Jetson Nano and Intel NUC for its superior balance of low cost, power efficiency, and processing capability).
* **Navigation Approach:** LiDAR-based SLAM utilizing ROS 2 Nav2 stack.
* **Localization Sensors:** RPLiDAR A1 + Odometry + MPU6050 IMU.
* **LLM:** **Google Gemini 3 Flash** (chosen for team familiarity, speed, multimodal capabilities, and a low-cost free tier for development).

---

## **5. Subsystem Architecture and Future Work**
The robot's software and hardware are decomposed into **seven functional subsystems**:
1. **Mobility:** Translates velocity commands to wheel motions.
2. **Navigation:** Builds maps, localizes the robot, and plans collision-free paths.
3. **Perception:** Captures audiovisual streams and checks for close-range obstacles.
4. **Intelligence:** Processes natural language via the Gemini API.
5. **Task Management:** Prioritizes hospital tasks, schedules deliveries, and coordinates goals.
6. **Interface & Communication:** Connects users to an Android tablet app, a web dashboard, and cloud services.
7. **Power Management:** Monitors the 12V battery and orders autonomous docking when low.

### **Areas of Improvement over Existing Tech:**
Unlike current commercial hospital robots that are highly proprietary and single-function, this project boasts an **open-source, multi-functional, cost-effective framework**. Furthermore, its design is explicitly localized for Oman, prioritizing Arabic language support and GCC hospital standards.

### **Implementation Roadmap (Future Work):**
* **Phase 1 (Months 5-6):** Hardware assembly and fine-tuning autonomous navigation in a lab.
* **Phase 2 (Month 7):** Integration of telepresence, tablet UI, and the Gemini API.
* **Phase 3 (Month 8):** Full system integration, multi-hour operation testing, and pilot trials in clinical environments.
* **Advanced Goals:** Future additions mention UV-C disinfection, electronic health record (EHR) integration, and emotional intelligence through facial and vocal prosody analysis.
